1 · The shared parser reads day-first dates month first (#1)

This is the bug #4471 was named for, and it covers 379 reports. Every tenant on file writes the day first, and the parser reads the month first, so 7 April arrives as 4 July. These reports came in through the app, by email, or through the booking-v2 form. The count includes the one-day moves (problem 3), because the desk files them here.

2 · The booking-v3 hint asks for month first (#2)

It covers 33 reports, all through the booking form of tenants that run booking-v3. That form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field; people type what it asks, and the form reads the value day first. The report reads like a parser report, so the subject rule merged it. The fix is the hint or the form, not the parser.

3 · Dates that move by one day (#3)

It covers 5 reports: the ones the desk itself listed as one-day moves in desk-notes.json. A Tuesday shows as Monday; one meeting lands on different days in Lisbon and Toronto. A day/month swap cannot do that. The likely cause is a time zone on the invite date (not confirmed). The desk files them under parser by tenant, so they stay counted there too; this row is what the queued parser fix will not close.

What the queue shows

  • The intake tag follows the browser, not the tenant. Intake tags a report hint when the reporter’s browser is en-US. The parser and the hint rows both carry both tags.
  • Same subject, different problem. Every subject line on the hint and one-day reports also appears on parser reports, so the subject rule could not have split them. Read the channel and the tenant’s form (causes.ts) before merging into a big ticket.
  • Keep the one-day list current. Problem 3 is only as complete as oneDayMove in desk-notes.json. New one-day reports belong there; this page picks them up on the next build.
